# Shared co-op postcard mailer (EDDM): a 25-day fast-cash evaluation

Research date: 2026-11-02. Window: collect revenue by 2026-11-27 (the Friday after
Thanksgiving, which is Thu 2026-11-26). Constraints: ≤ $300 at risk before money
arrives, ≤ $3,000 committed in total. The operator could be in any US metro.

Labels: **PUBLISHED** means a figure from a cited page. **ESTIMATE** means my own
inference, and its basis is stated. A **VENDOR CLAIM** comes from a company that sells
courses or software to postcard operators. Those companies have a conflict of interest,
so treat these figures as the optimistic end of the range.

---

## Verdict (read this first)

- **Viable as a presale. Hard to make big in 25 days.** Advertisers pay before anything
  is printed, so the cash at risk before revenue is small: about $0–$150 for
  mock-ups, plus card-processing fees if you have to refund. That fits the $300 limit.
- **A 10,000-home 9x12 card costs $4,100–$5,800 to print and mail.** That is above the
  **$3,000 total-commitment cap**. It only works if advertisers' prepaid money pays for
  the print and postage, and you never advance your own money. If the cap is meant
  literally, run **5,000 homes** instead, which costs about $2,100–$2,600.
- **Realistic result for a first card.** Expect about $1,000–$3,000 contribution if it
  sells out. $5,000 from one card needs about 16–18 slots at about $600 each, which is
  the top of the evidenced price range. No published operator data shows that happening
  on a first card in under 2 weeks.
- **The main risk is how fast the card fills, not cost.** Vendor sources say a
  beginner's first card takes **4–6 weeks** to fill. This plan needs it filled in
  **about 10 days** (by Thu 2026-11-12) for the mail to arrive in early December.
- **The revenue date and the in-home date are different.** Money is collected at the
  presale, so revenue by Nov 27 is feasible. Mail would reach homes around **Dec 1–11**,
  so holiday offers should target December (catering, gift cards, year-end), not Black
  Friday.

**Correction to the brief:** 200 pieces is the **minimum** for a Retail mailing, not a
daily limit. The Retail limit is **5,000 per ZIP per day**. A 10,000-home Retail mailing
therefore needs either 2+ ZIPs (each drop at its own post office) or 2 drop days.

**Retail or BMEU?** Retail costs $2,600 per 10,000. BMEU costs $2,590 per 10,000 plus
$780 in fees in the first year. **Use Retail** (ESTIMATE, arithmetic from the rows above).

**Practical rules (PUBLISHED: USPS EDDM page).**
- Select whole carrier routes in the EDDM online tool and pay online.
- The piece carries the EDDM Retail indicia and "Postal Customer" instead of names.
- Bundle the cards in 50–100s with facing slips.
- Drop them at the post office that serves those routes.
- You cannot mail part of a route.

## 4. Who buys, when, and how slots are sold

- **Categories (PUBLISHED, operator reports):**
  - Missoula: dentists (the most consistent, largest-spot buyer), HVAC, body contouring
    and med spa, roofers, restaurants and coffee shops ("make the card sticky"), home
    services, breweries.
  - Making Sense of Cents adds plumbing, landscaping, painting, realtors and auto repair.
  - Rule used everywhere: **one business per category** (the Cornelius rate card;
    Missoula).
- **Holiday timing (ESTIMATE, no operator data found).** Mail arriving in early December
  suits:
  - restaurants (catering, gift cards)
  - salons and spas (gift certificates)
  - dentists (use-it-or-lose-it year-end insurance benefits, a common dental marketing
    angle)
  - HVAC (heating tune-ups)
  - auto (winter service)

  It does not suit lawn care or landscaping.
- **Selling methods (PUBLISHED):**
  - Missoula: Facebook Messenger and email first, then a phone call to anyone who
    replies. No cold calling. Some operators go door to door. They have sold to towns
    hours away remotely. Advertisers **pay up front**, and they now sell 3–6 month
    prepaid packages.
  - Making Sense of Cents: printing starts only after all 16 spots are paid.
  - 9x12 Tools: email-first outreach, 50 new prospects a day, follow-ups on Day 0/2/5/9.
    This is a recommendation, not measured data.
    https://www.9x12tools.com/blog/first-50-prospects-a-day-outreach-plan
- **Time to fill and close rates:**
  - **Close rates: none published.** I found no operator data on visits or calls per
    sold slot.
  - Time to fill, as claimed by vendors and operators:
    - first card, beginners: 4–6 weeks; later cards: 1–2 weeks (Making Sense of Cents)
    - XponentSYS: about 2 weeks per zone
    - 9x12 Tools: 50–100 businesses contacted to fill a first card at 10–15 hours/week
      (VENDOR CLAIM): https://www.9x12tools.com/postcard-marketing-business
    - one vendor warns of 6–8 weeks of selling before a mail date
- **Contacts per sold slot (ESTIMATE).** The vendor claim of 50–100 contacts for about
  16 slots implies a 16–32% close rate. That is plausible only for warm or inbound leads.
  For cold walk-ins I assume a **5–10% close rate**, so about **10–20 conversations per
  sold slot** and **150–300 conversations for 15 slots**. At about 8 walk-in
  conversations an hour in dense retail strips, that is about 20–40 hours of selling.
  This is achievable in 10 days only if those hours are actually worked.

## 5. Unit economics

Common inputs:
- Postage: $0.26/piece (PUBLISHED).
- Bundling supplies: about $50 (ESTIMATE).
- Design: $0–$100, using Canva and self-built ads (ESTIMATE; Cornelius charges
  advertisers $75 for design, which is a possible extra revenue line).
- Card processing: about 3% of revenue (ESTIMATE, at Stripe-type pricing of about
  2.9% + $0.30).

**A. 10,000 homes, 9x12, Retail EDDM, self-managed (bundling and drop yourself)**

| | Low | High |
|---|---|---|
| Print | $1,483 | $2,399 |
| Postage | $2,600 | $2,600 |
| Supplies + design | $50 | $150 |
| **Fixed cost** | **$4,133** | **$5,149** |
| Full-service alternative (print + postage + bundling + drop) | $5,799 (9x12method) | |

Slots needed, at net-of-fees prices (ESTIMATE):

| Slot price | Break-even | +$3,000 contribution | +$5,000 contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| $350 | 13–16 | 21–24 (more than one card holds) | not feasible |
| $450 | 10–12 | 17–19 | 21–24 (more than one card holds) |
| $595 | 8–9 | 13–15 | 16–18 |

A full card of 18 slots at $450 brings in $8,100, which nets about $2,700–$3,700.
**$5,000 from one card needs about $600 slots and a near-full card.**

**B. 5,000 homes, 9x12, Retail (the only version within a literal $3,000 total cap)**

- Fixed cost: print $744–$1,309 + postage $1,300 + about $50–$150 = **about
  $2,100–$2,760**.
- Slot price at 5,000 homes: about **$250–$350** (ESTIMATE; between the $150 per 2,500
  Missoula price and the $477 median ask).
- Slots needed at $300:
  - break-even: 8–10
  - +$3,000 contribution: 18–20, which is a full card
  - +$5,000: not feasible on one card

**Cash timing.**
- Collect by card or invoice **before** ordering print. Printers and USPS EDDM Retail
  are prepaid, so this works if your sale-to-print gap is a few days.
- Stripe-type processors may hold payouts on a brand-new account. Leave a buffer of
  several days, or take ACH or Zelle from advertisers you know.
- Example schedule for 2026:
  - Sell 11/2–11/12.
  - Order print on 11/12–13 at a 5–7 business-day turnaround; cards ship around
    11/20–24.
  - Drop at the post office 11/23–12/1.
  - Mail reaches homes around **12/1–12/11**.

**Legal and practical issues.**
- **Mail rules:**
  - Whole routes only.
  - At most 5,000 pieces per ZIP per day at Retail.
  - The piece must be a flat within the size limits above and weigh ≤ 3.3 oz.
  - Use the correct EDDM Retail indicia.
  - Advertising content only, no personal correspondence. Do not include lottery or
    gambling-for-consideration offers (federal mail law).
- **Ad claims:** each advertiser warrants its offer is truthful and lawful (FTC Act
  truth-in-advertising). Add that to the order terms. Regulated categories carry extra
  rules, such as contractor license numbers in some states and real-estate brokerage
  disclosures. Make each advertiser responsible for its own compliance.
- **Refund policy (threshold presale):** state in writing "Card prints only if N spots
  are paid by DATE; otherwise a full refund within 2 business days." The processor
  usually keeps its fee on a refund, so budget about 3% of refunded money as a loss.
- **Business setup:** local business licence, and whether your state taxes advertising
  or printing sales. Both vary by state, so check before the first sale. Neither should
  block a 25-day test.
- **Competition:** Valpak and Money Mailer franchises operate in most metros, at about
  $250–$650 per 10,000 (anecdotal). Category exclusivity and a lower first-mailing price
  are the usual differentiators.

## 6. Risks and kill tests

| Risk | Mitigation / kill test |
|---|---|
| Card does not fill in time (the main risk) | **Kill test 1:** by **Fri 11/6**, at least 3 *paid* slots out of at least 60 conversations. If not, shrink to 5,000 homes or a 6.25x11 card, or stop. |
| | **Kill test 2:** by **Thu 11/12**, paid slots must cover the fixed cost × 1.15 (about 11 slots at $450 for 10,000 homes, or about 9 at $300 for 5,000). If not, **refund everyone and cancel.** Loss is about 3% of collected money plus $0–$150 in mock-ups, which is under $300. |
| Advancing your own money | **Rule:** no print order and no postage purchase until cash collected ≥ fixed cost. Never front postage. |
| Holiday mail delay | Drop no later than 12/1. Sell December-dated offers (valid through 12/31), not Black-Friday-only ones. |
| Printer prices differ from published ones | Get a live quote for the exact spec on Day 1, before quoting slot prices. |
| Advertisers feel a $450–$600 ask is high for an unproven card | Sell a "founding advertiser" price of about $350–$400 with category lock, or offer 5,000 homes at about $250–$300. Missoula moved to a lower price on a smaller card for this reason. |
| Second-card income is unproven (renewal of about 60–70% is one operator's self-report) | Pre-sell December + January packages at a discount, paid up front, only after the first card actually mails. |
| Processor hold or chargebacks | Use clear written terms, a proof-approval step for each ad, and prompt refunds if the threshold is missed. |

**Bottom line.** This is a legitimate, low-risk presale model that fits the $300
at-risk limit. Expected first-card contribution is **about $1,000–$3,000**, and in a
25-day window **$5,000 is unlikely**. A 10,000-home card only fits the $3,000 total cap
if advertisers' money pays for everything. Whether it works comes down to selling about
11–18 prepaid slots in about 10 days, and no published data supports that pace for a
beginner.
