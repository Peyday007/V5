# Remote brokerage / lead-gen for contractor-performed services: research (2026-11-03)

Scope: the owner sells **demand** (customer requests or booked jobs) to independent contractors, who do all physical work. The owner runs ads, landing pages, routing and billing online. Each figure is tagged **PUBLISHED** (stated by a source, URL given) or **ESTIMATE** (my inference). Most per-lead prices come from vendor blogs with a commercial interest, so I treat them as weaker than primary data.

## 2. Who pays the broker and how

### Per-lead prices contractors already pay (evidence buyers pay)
- **Service Direct (exclusive phone leads), junk removal:** national average CPL "around $33.75" (H1 2023), Florida "closer to $42.50". Contractors set their own CPL. Only exclusive phone leads are sold. Billing is by credit card for "Billable Leads", plus a monthly subscription and a "Billing Threshold", because "the company pays for ads before charging for leads". PUBLISHED: https://blog.servicedirect.com/how-much-do-service-direct-junk-removal-leads-cost ; https://support.servicedirect.com/how-you-are-billed ; https://support.servicedirect.com/billing-center
- **99 Calls junk removal leads (100% exclusive):** Google Ads leads $39–$87, LSA $39–$120 (10th–90th percentile nationally), SEO leads $12.99, "no contract", $99/mo hosting. PUBLISHED (vendor): https://99calls.com/Junk-Removal-Leads.htm
- **Angi (shared, sold to 3–8 pros):** ~$15–$120 per lead depending on trade. Third-party vendor blogs only, so PUBLISHED-weak: https://www.getnextphone.com/blog/angi-leads-for-contractors ; https://wwwleadtruffleco.pages.dev/blog/angi-leads-cost-pricing-contractors-2026
- **Angi network size:** ~168,000 transacting professionals in Q4 2024 (FY2024 10-K). Third-party summaries say average monthly active pros fell 19% to ~122,000 in 2025, so many pros are leaving shared-lead marketplaces. PUBLISHED: https://capedge.com/filing/1705110/0001705110-25-000018/ANGI-10K-2024FY ; https://www.panabee.com/news/angi-earnings-2025-annual (summary, unverified). ESTIMATE: this is a large pool of contractors who buy leads and dislike shared leads, which is the pitch for "exclusive, pay per lead, no contract".
- **Thumbtack:** pros are charged "the moment the customer reaches out", with weekly budgets as caps. Third-party ranges are house cleaning $5–20 and most trades $10–100+. PUBLISHED (community/third-party): https://community.thumbtack.com/discussion/656/how-are-lead-prices-calculated ; https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/what-is-thumbtack-how-it-works/
- **Junk removal pay-per-call networks:** $15–$40 per qualified call, 60–120 s minimum duration. PUBLISHED: https://callscaler.com/marketplace/junk-removal ; OfferVault junk offer $2–45/call: https://newui.offervault.com/offer/cad0d20184c43a34a1a3360d4056b26b/junk-removal-pay-per-call-us . Payment is commonly net-30 (2024 roundup): https://fbverse.pages.dev/blog/best-pay-per-call-networks
- **1-800-GOT-JUNK affiliate:** 2.4% of a completed, paid job. PUBLISHED: https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/1-800-got-junk-affiliate-program . Uneconomic.

### Commission / referral rates (holiday lighting)
- Holiday Light Co (AZ): 1099 commission-only, 10% on new installs and 15% on returning customers. PUBLISHED: https://www.mediabistro.com/jobs/1995918984-seasonal-sales-account-executive
- Humbug Holiday Lighting: $15/hr plus 30% of gross profit. PUBLISHED: https://www.career.com/job/humbug-holiday-lighting/christmas-light-installer/j202509211247138188769
- Installer roles at 8–12% of sales; Mosquito Hunters/holiday at 5%. PUBLISHED: https://ph-hhl-careers.careerplug.com/jobs/3104918
- Average holiday-light install job: $443 (range $220–683). PUBLISHED: https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/seasonal/install-holiday-lighting ; HomeGuide $240–650: https://homeguide.com/costs/christmas-light-installation-cost
- ESTIMATE: a 10% referral on a $443 job is ~$44, about the same as an exclusive lead price, but it is paid after the install and after the customer pays.

### Cash timing
- The industry norm is a **card charged at lead delivery** (Thumbtack, Service Direct) or a prepaid wallet (e.g., Baton requires a $200 minimum balance: https://intercom.help/baton-leads-inc/en/articles/13687283-baton-s-prepaid-billing-model-effective-january-31-2026). PUBLISHED.
- ESTIMATE: copy that model. Use a Stripe card-on-file or a prepaid "5-lead pack" invoice. Stripe card payouts usually settle in ~2 business days for an established account (a new account may hold the first payout ~7 days), so a lead sold by ~Nov 18 is settled cash by Nov 27.
- Commission-on-completion lands too late and needs trust and enforcement. Reject it as the billing model.

## 4. Contractor supply (buyers of leads)
- **Find them remotely:** Google Maps listings (search "junk removal" in each metro: few reviews and no website or ads usually means hungry for work), state licence lookups where a licence exists (e.g., CSLB in CA), and Angi/Thumbtack/Yelp profiles (proof they already buy leads). Junk removal and holiday lighting are mostly unlicensed trades, so Maps and Yelp are the main sources. ESTIMATE.
- **Willingness to buy:** shown by the Angi network (~122–168k pros), Service Direct and 99 Calls selling exclusive leads, and Thumbtack's per-contact model (PUBLISHED, above). The pitch writes itself: "exclusive, no contract, pay per lead, credit for bad numbers", and it lands while Angi pros are churning. ESTIMATE.
- **Holiday-light supply is the binding constraint:** installers are "fully booked by mid-November" in DFW and "booked completely after mid-October" in Virginia Beach, and late bookings carry a 20–40% premium. PUBLISHED (installer marketing): https://christmas-lights-experts-dfw.pages.dev/blog-when-to-book ; https://outdoorlights.com/virginia-beach/resources/blog/2024/october/why-booking-your-holiday-lighting-appointment-in . ESTIMATE: by Nov 3–10 most good installers need no leads. The few with capacity are new or small crews, the lowest-trust buyers.
- **Commercial snow:** "By October, most commercial clients have already signed contracts". PUBLISHED: https://niagarastandsout.ca/blogs/news/snow-removal-leads-ontario-commercial-residential-marketing ; contracts are signed July–September per https://theguyoutdoorservices.pages.dev/blog/commercial-snow-removal-planning-guide . Reject.

## 5. Legal
- **Interstate household-goods brokers** must register with FMCSA (49 U.S.C. §13904). FMCSA says the limited-service exclusion is not a "mechanism ... to evade regulatory oversight". PUBLISHED: https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/consumer-protection/what-difference-between-household-goods-hhg-mover-and-hhg-broker ; https://govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-04-01/pdf/2013-07461.pdf . **Moving is excluded.**
- **Home-improvement salesperson licensing:** Maryland requires an MHIC salesperson licence (with an exam) for anyone who "sells or offers to sell" home improvement on behalf of a licensed contractor. PUBLISHED: https://labor.maryland.gov/license/mhic/ . ESTIMATE: other states (NJ, NY localities, etc.) have similar rules. **Mitigation:** sell *leads to contractors* (B2B data/advertising) rather than quoting, negotiating or signing the homeowner on the contractor's behalf, avoid commission-per-sale structures, and avoid MD (plus any state with a salesperson licence) for home-improvement categories. Junk removal and holiday decorating are generally not "home improvement" (ESTIMATE; unverified state by state).
- **TCPA:** the FCC one-to-one consent rule was vacated by the 11th Circuit on 2025-01-24, so standard prior-express-consent rules apply. PUBLISHED: https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/250130-eleventh-circuit-vacates-fcc-s-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-rule . ESTIMATE: still put clear consent language on the form naming "a local [service] provider", text or call only consumers who submitted, never auto-text contractors' cell phones cold, and keep the consent log. A 2025–26 FCC follow-up was not found (UNRESOLVED).
- **CAN-SPAM:** B2B cold email to contractors is legal with a truthful header and subject, a physical address and a working opt-out. ESTIMATE from the FTC guide: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business
- **Consumer honesty:** the landing page must disclose that it is a referral service matching the consumer with independent providers, not the provider itself. ESTIMATE (FTC deception principles).

## 6. Unit economics (junk removal, per metro) — ESTIMATE
- Sale price: $35–45 per exclusive lead (Service Direct average $33.75; 99 Calls $39–87).
- Facebook lead-form CPL $20–41, plus 15–25% invalid leads credited back. Effective cost per sellable lead is ~$25–50.
- Margin per sold lead: −$15 to +$20. Break-even is plausible, profit is not assured.
- Google Search CPL $90–120 is a loss. Use Google only for exact-match, late-night, local "junk removal near me" if the CPC is under $5.
- Holiday lights: a lead could sell for $50–75 to an installer with open slots (job $443, 10–15% referral comparables), with Facebook CPL $20–70. Better margin, but few buyers.
- **Deals possible by Nov 27:** 2–4 contractors × 5–15 leads each = 10–60 leads × $40 = **$400–2,400 gross**, against $300–1,200 ad spend. Modal outcome: about 1–2 paying contractors and $200–600 gross.
- **Costs:** domain + landing (Carrd/Google Sites, free–$19), call-tracking number (Twilio is a paid API, excluded; use a free Google Voice number per metro, or route form leads by email/SMS from the owner's existing stack), Stripe (2.9% + 30¢), Craigslist $5/post, ad spend ≤$300 before first income.
- **Owner hours:** ~10–15 h setup, then ~1 h/day (contractor calls are the bottleneck; AI cannot close a contractor by phone).

### What AI automates
Ad copy and creative variants, landing pages per metro and category, contractor prospect lists from Maps/Yelp, personalised cold emails and follow-up sequences, lead qualification, instant SMS/email to the matched contractor (from the existing stack, no new paid API), invoicing and refund-credit logic, and daily CPL/close reporting. Not automatable: contractor trust and the first "yes" (often a phone call), handling disputed leads, and Meta account review.

## 7. Exact execution path (rank 1, with the rank-2 test inside it)
1. **Day 1–2:** pick 4–6 metros with high junk demand and haulers that don't advertise (ESTIMATE candidates: Sun Belt suburbs such as Atlanta, Charlotte, Tampa, Phoenix, Dallas-FW; avoid MD). Scrape 30–50 haulers per metro from Google Maps and Yelp, and use AI to draft personalised outreach: "exclusive junk leads, $39 each, card charged only when delivered, bad numbers credited, no contract; first 2 leads free."
2. **Day 2–3:** build one landing page per metro/category with a lead form, consent text, a referral-service disclosure and a privacy policy. Launch Facebook lead-form ads at $15–25/day per metro across 2 metros only (spend kept ≤$300 until income). Post Craigslist services ads at $5 each in the same metros. For lights, add a "last-minute install before Thanksgiving" variant only in metros where an installer confirms open slots.
3. **Day 3–6:** deliver 2 free leads to each contractor who replies, then put their card on file via a Stripe payment link or prepaid 5-lead pack ($195). Route each lead to exactly one contractor within 60 s.
4. **Day 7–18:** scale only the metro/category where cost per sellable lead is under 75% of the sale price. Pause the rest. Sell overflow calls to a pay-per-call network only as a fallback (net-30 lands after the deadline).
5. **Last sale for settled cash:** about Nov 20 (allowing Stripe settlement).

## 8. Kill test
- **Day 5 (Nov 8):** ≥2 contractors have a card on file or have prepaid. Otherwise kill: the buyers don't exist for a stranger.
- **Day 7 (Nov 10):** Facebook cost per *sellable* lead is ≤ $30 in at least one metro after ≥$100 spend. Otherwise kill, with total loss ≤ ~$150.
- **Day 10:** ≥ 70% of delivered leads are accepted without a credit request. Otherwise kill.
- **Hard stop:** cumulative ad spend minus collected cash > $300 → stop (owner's cap).

## 9. Key uncertainties
- Junk lead prices are from 2023 vendor blogs. The 2026 price a hauler will pay a *new, unknown* broker is likely lower ($20–30, ESTIMATE).
- No reliable Facebook CPL for junk removal specifically. The $41 home-improvement average may not hold for a new account in its learning phase.
- New Stripe and Meta accounts may hold payouts or cap spend.
- Per-state licensing for "lead seller" versus "salesperson" is not verified beyond MD.
- Holiday-installer capacity in November is unknown per metro. The evidence says most are booked.
