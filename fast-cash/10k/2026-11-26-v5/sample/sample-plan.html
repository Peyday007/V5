SAMPLE. Fictional business and fictional investor, written to show the scope, structure and financial depth of our E-2 plans. Market statistics are real and cited; every figure about the business itself is stated as the investor's assumption. Not legal advice; the attorney of record decides how the plan is used.

Ridgeline Coffee Roasters LLC

E-2 Treaty Investor Business Plan · Asheville, North Carolina · Prepared for: Eleanor Whitcombe (United Kingdom national), Managing Member

1. Executive summary

Ridgeline Coffee Roasters LLC will open a 1,600 sq ft café with an on-site small-batch roastery in Asheville, Buncombe County, NC. The investor, a UK national, has irrevocably committed $210,000 of personal funds, of which $189,000 is spent on build-out, equipment, inventory and deposits before opening, and $21,000 is held as working capital. The business has two revenue lines: café sales and wholesale roasted coffee to local restaurants and offices from month 4. Projected revenue grows from $534,764 in Year 1 to $1,027,687 in Year 5. The plan creates 10.5 full-time-equivalent U.S. jobs by the end of Year 5, with Year-5 payroll of $453,715, well beyond supporting the investor and her family.

2. The investor and the investment

Ownership: Eleanor Whitcombe holds 100% of the membership interest (≥50% required for E-2 treaty-nationality control). Source of funds: proceeds of the sale of her UK residence and personal savings, documented separately by bank statements and the completion statement (to be supplied by the client; the plan references them, it does not substitute for them).

3. Market analysis

Buncombe County had an estimated 279,210 residents in 2024, up from 276,789 in 2023 (U.S. Census Bureau, Population Estimates, Vintage 2024). The county had 124 snack and non-alcoholic beverage bars (NAICS 722515, which includes coffee shops), employing 1,406 people with annual payroll of $33.0 million (Census County Business Patterns 2023): one such establishment per 2,252 residents, and average payroll of $23,440 per employee against $21,247 for the same industry nationally (84,105 establishments, 978,901 employees; county-level sum, CBP 2023). The local industry pays above the national average, which the hiring plan reflects. Tourism-driven demand, a dense independent-restaurant base for wholesale accounts, and competition from established local roasters are discussed with the investor's site-specific evidence (foot-traffic counts and letters of intent from wholesale prospects are listed in the exhibits the client supplies).

4. Operations and competitive position

Opening hours 6:30–18:00, 360 days a year. Positioning: single-origin and house-roasted espresso, roastery visible from the counter, wholesale beans delivered weekly. Competitors are identified by name in a full plan with their price points; this sample omits them because the business is fictional.

4a. Marketing and sales plan

Café: opening-week tasting events, a loyalty app, Google Business Profile and local listings, and seasonal menus timed to the tourist calendar. Wholesale: the roaster/wholesale coordinator calls on independent restaurants, offices and inns, offering a two-week free trial bag program; target 4 accounts by month 12 (investor's assumption, reflected in the wholesale revenue line). Marketing budget: $900 a month.

4b. SWOT and risks

Assessment
StrengthsHouse roasting gives margin control and a wholesale line competitors without roasters lack.
WeaknessesNew brand; single site; investor's first U.S. venture.
OpportunitiesWholesale to the county's independent restaurants; online bean subscriptions (not in the projections).
ThreatsEstablished local roasters; seasonality of tourist traffic; green-coffee price swings.
MitigationWorking capital of $21,000 plus positive operating cash flow from Year 1; green coffee bought on 3-month contracts.

4c. Management

The investor will direct and develop the enterprise as Managing Member and General Manager. Her CV (to be supplied by the client) is summarized here in a full plan, with the managerial experience relevant to running a food-service business.

6. Marginality analysis

A business is marginal if it only supports the investor and family. Ridgeline employs 6 FTE at the end of Year 1 and 10.5 at the end of Year 5, with Year-5 payroll of $453,715 and Year-5 net income of $32,572. Its capacity to generate income and U.S. employment substantially exceeds a minimal living for the investor.

7.5 Break-even

Year-1 fixed costs including payroll of $26,505 a month and a café contribution margin of 66% put break-even at about $39,978 of monthly café revenue, about 159 transactions a day at a $8 average ticket.

7.6 Key assumptions (investor's)

Average ticket $8 rising 2.5% a year; café transactions ramp from 110 to 230 a day in Year 1, then grow 8%, 6%, 5%, 4%; café COGS 31%, wholesale COGS 45%; rent $5,200 a month rising 3% a year; card fees 2.7%; employer payroll taxes and benefits 11%. Full monthly model: financials.csv.
