## 3. Quantitative evaluation (correlated)

**Model.** `fast-cash/10k/plan-model-2026-11-09.ts`, output in
`plan-model-2026-11-09-output.txt`. It simulates 20,000 worlds per strategy.

- **Outreach.** Brain's share of the 945 sends a day is 150–600, because the
  owner's own campaigns share the accounts.
- **Start date.** The first send happens 1–6 days out: it waits on the owner's
  grant.
- **Counting.** Replies are drawn as a Poisson count and closes as a Binomial
  count, so small numbers keep their real noise.
- **Timing.** Each line has its own last useful send day. For gifts that is the
  Dec 5–18 cut-offs; for invoices it is the settlement lag.
- **Money.** Cash is reported gross (what the ledger counts) and net (after
  supplier cost and 3% fees).

**Correlated failures.** One reply-rate factor, one positive-share factor and
one credibility factor are drawn once per world and applied to every line.
There is also a 15% chance of a deliverability incident that cuts 30–80% of all
later volume at once. The lines share one mailbox pool, one sender reputation
and one unknown seller, so they rise and fall together.

A naive model that redraws each line independently gives a narrower spread
(8.0% vs 9.2% reach for the lead strategy). Its downside looks safer than it
is: when the shared factor is bad, every line is bad at once.

| Strategy | Gross p10 | p50 | p90 | Net p50 | Reach $10K (published ranges) | Reach $10K (**measured** rates) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Gifts first, AI setups second** | $0 | $2,728 | $9,623 | $561 | **9.2%** (Wilson lower 8.8%) | **0.2%** |
| Gifts first, cleaning second | $0 | $2,508 | $8,769 | $467 | 7.2% | 0.2% |
| Gifts first + $300 lead ads | $91 | $2,859 | $9,512 | $385 | 8.8% | 0.2% |
| Balanced five lines | $0 | $1,868 | $6,880 | $480 | 3.6% | 0.0% |
| AI setups first | $0 | $2,018 | $7,200 | $623 | 4.0% | 0.1% |

**More lines.** Adding sources helps only where they do not share the binding
factor. All five lines here share the reply rate, so spreading thinly lowers
reach. The plan therefore concentrates on the line with the largest ticket and
the earliest cut-off (gifts), keeps a second line, and holds the small lines as
fillers.

**Net vs gross.** Gift brokerage turns $10K gross into only about $1.3–2K net.
If the owner means $10K *net*, no tested strategy exceeds 0.4%.

### The rebuilt plan, as Brain states it

1. **Day 0: change how deals are sold, not how many emails go out.**
   - Sell each small-ticket offer as a bundle (about nine units).
   - Ask for a deposit or prepayment at signing.
   - Have suppliers paid from the buyer's payment, not the owner's $3,000.
   - These three changes take the plan from 4.2% to 75.4% coverage. All three
     are assumptions, so the first sales must measure them: the bundle close
     rate, deposit acceptance, and supplier terms.
2. **Do not buy sending capacity now.** On this pool, volume is not the
   constraint. The $375 before-proof limit is better held for a measured
   winner.
3. **Search for different openings.** The openings held cap out at 6.3% even at
   their best. The next goal round is asked for buyers already asking, at $500+
   per deal, payable inside 24 days, in any industry.
4. **The old Instantly record does not bind this plan.** Production had no
   Instantly key, so the record could not be read on this run. On the regression
   case it is `CONTEXT_ONLY`. If the key is set, it is read as the as-is floor
   and printed beside the plan.

### What a 90% plan still requires

- **Remove the three assumptions** by measuring them in the first sales:
  - the bundle close rate;
  - the deposit acceptance rate;
  - supplier terms that allow prepaid fulfilment.
- **Add openings whose own demand is larger or faster.** The pool as held is
  structurally limited to 6.3%.
- Until both happen, the plan is coverage of stated ranges and assumptions, and
  Brain says so.
