The planner's per-leg figures are *net of direct cost*. They do not deduct outreach cost or payment fees, and the plan says so. The portfolio net is **capped at the $10,000 gap**, which is why its p50 and p90 both read exactly $10,000.

### 2.3 Arithmetic

**Simulation chain, per leg and per world** (`server/services/goals/portfolio.ts`):

```
contacted  = contacts_per_day × leg_share × (days_left − days_to_cash)
interested ~ Poisson(contacted × reply_rate)
deals      ~ Binomial(interested, close_rate), capped at volume
cash       = deals × price  (net = deals × (price − direct cost))
```

**Leg 1 at the planner's medians:**

```
interested ≈ 4,314 × 0.076   ≈ 328
deals      ≈ 328 × 0.26      ≈ 85, before the volume cap
```

The median reported was 27 deals. The draws are uniform, not set at their centres, and the volume cap binds, so a simple product overstates the median.

Even at p10, 4 deals × $10,000 = $40,000. That is why the leg "covers" the target in almost every world.

**What has to be true for leg 1 alone to reach $10,000:**
- 1 deal at $10,000 settles before 2026-12-09.
- The buyer GitHub would therefore have to pay $10,000 up front for a Brain-produced spreadsheet, sold by cold email.

## PART 3. Audit of the 98% figure

### 3.1 Method

- **Simulation:** a Monte Carlo of **2,000 worlds** (`PORTFOLIO_WORLDS = 2_000`, seed 20261103). The reconstruction explored 11 variants.
- **Input distributions:**
  - An input with evidence is drawn uniformly over its stated low–high range.
  - An input with no value is drawn uniformly over a default range: contacts a day 10–930, conversion 0–8%, volume 1–200.
  - An *unestablished* input is clipped to the unfavourable half of its range (price LOW, days HIGH, cost HIGH). The $10,000 price was labelled "E", so it was **not** clipped.
- **How probabilities were assigned:** they were not assigned. Every unknown is a flat range. No input is calibrated against an outcome.
- **Dependencies:** none are modelled. The legs, and the inputs inside each leg, are drawn **independently**. Two consequences:
  - No correlation was modelled between legs 2, 4 and 6 (one buyer type, "domain buyer or seller") or between legs 3 and 5 ("small business operators").
  - The legs are counted as six, although they describe two buyer groups plus the GitHub item.
- **Result:**
  - Reach 0.98 in the leading combination. The reconstruction baseline is 0.987, labelled `COVERAGE`.
  - The floor is 0.675. That is the reach that survives the planner's own adverse variants.
  - The binding constraint is VOLUME.
  - The reply rate required for the plan to reach the target is 0.926% (`requiredReply = 0.00926`).

### 3.3 A, B and C, kept separate

- **A. Model probability: 97.95%–98.7%.**
  - This is the share of 2,000 simulated worlds in which settled cash reached $10,000.
  - Brain's own label is "coverage of assumed inputs, not a probability".
- **B. The evidence behind A.**
  - Almost all of A comes from one input: a $10,000 price labelled "estimated from evidence".
  - That figure is the reward GitHub pays security researchers for a *critical vulnerability finding*. It is not a price GitHub pays a vendor for a spreadsheet or for programming. See Part 4.
  - The buyer is a single company, modelled as a contactable population with up to 200 repeat purchases.
  - Reply rate, close rate, volume, channel, fulfiller and cash timing are all unestablished.
  - No outcome data exists for this goal.
  - Brain has fewer than 10 accepted plans, so it cannot calibrate.
  - **B therefore does not support A.**
- **C. Defensible real-world probability.**
  - **The model cannot justify one, and this audit does not produce one.**
  - Brain withheld it ("withheld: no calibrated source"), and that was correct.
  - The plain qualitative statement that *can* be defended: the leg that supplies the 98% rests on a misread price. With that leg removed, Brain's own model gives a median of $432 against a $10,000 target. So the real-world chance of this plan collecting $10,000 by 2026-12-09 is **not 98%**, and is not shown by any evidence to be meaningfully above zero.
  - A numeric probability is not stated, because none can be justified.

Brain's p50 of 27 critical "deals" ($270K) for one newcomer in about 12 days equals roughly a third of GitHub's entire 2023 bounty spend. Its p90 ($947K) is more than all of it.
- **The offer text is corrupted.** It reads "programming for 'Deliver exactly what the published request asks for Software Advice analyzed data from 4 4…'", which is an HVAC software-buyer statistic (clm_36a246…) spliced into a vulnerability bounty. The deliverable is a *spreadsheet* (PRODUCE_SPREADSHEET). GitHub pays only for security vulnerabilities, and a spreadsheet is not one.

### 1.3 Is uniform sampling over guessed ranges a probability?
No. Each unestablished input (reply 0–~15%, close 0–~52%, contacts 10–930 a day, volume 1–200) is a range the planner chose, sampled uniformly. Uniform is itself a strong prior that nobody justified. The output is the share of a box of assumptions that clears $10K. Move the box and the "probability" moves with it, and no observed data constrains where the box sits. Brain says as much ("COVERAGE… not a probability"; "too few finished accepted plans (under 10) to calibrate"). Reporting it as "reaches the target in 98% of simulated scenarios" invites exactly the misreading this review is asked to check. The clipping rule (an unknown is held no better than its range centre) does not fix this: when the centre itself is 10–60× too high, as shown below, clipping at the centre is still optimistic.

The figures are also inconsistent with each other. The portfolio net p10 is $368, while the "adverse" 10th percentile is $32,266. One body of output cannot hold both readings.

### 1.4 Reply and close rates under evidence
- **Brain's rates.** Reply 0.076 × close 0.26 means **1.98% of contacts buy**.
- **Evidence on total replies.** Total cold-email reply rates are about 2–6% (Instantly's own guidance is 5–10% as "solid", citing Belkins at mid single digits over 16.5M emails: [Instantly benchmarks](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks)).

That is **≈0.64% interested replies per send** (summarised via [serpsculpt / search results](https://serpsculpt.com/average-cold-email-response-rate-b2b/); secondary).
- **Buyers per send.** For an unknown sender with an unsolicited low-ticket offer, 5–20% of interested replies paying is an assumption, not evidence. It gives **0.03%–0.2% of sends buying**, which is 10–60× below Brain's rate.

### 1.5 Is leg-level independence defensible?
No, for four reasons:
1. **One sender.** All legs draw on the same owner sending infrastructure, but `drawDemandWorlds` draws `perDay` separately for each leg (`sampler(contactsPerDay, rng)` per opening). Capacity should be a single shared draw split among the legs, not six independent draws.
2. **One reputation.** Reply and close rates share common drivers: domain reputation and deliverability, the same unproven operator, the same lack of a payment link, the same season. They are positively correlated.
3. **Duplicates.** Three legs are the same "domain buyer or seller" market at $75 and two are the same "small business operators" market at $9. Counting them as independent legs double-counts one demand pool.
4. **It hardly matters here.** Correlation only matters when several legs carry weight, and here one leg carries it all. Correlating the legs moved the reconstruction from 96.4% to 93.9%. The real defect is structural (§1.2), not the correlation.

"""Approximate replica of Brain's drawDemandWorlds for the six-leg candidate, plus
re-runs under realistic reply/close rates and a correlated-market variant.
Inputs not visible in the extraction (ranges, shares) are reconstructed and flagged."""
import math, random

W = 20000
DAYS_LEFT = 16

# Reconstructed legs: (name, price, cash_days_range, volume_range, share)
# share calibrated so GitHub contacted p50 ~ 4,314 (brief); remaining legs split the rest.
LEGS = [
    ("github", 10000, (3.87, 3.87), (1, 200), 0.76),
    ("dom75a", 75, (3.87, 3.87), (1, 200), 0.05),
    ("biz9a", 9, (3.87, 3.87), (1, 200), 0.05),
    ("dom75b", 75, (8, 21), (1, 200), 0.05),
    ("biz9b", 9, (8, 21), (1, 200), 0.05),
    ("dom75c", 75, (8, 21), (1, 1), 0.04),
]

def poisson(m, r):
    if m <= 0: return 0
    if m < 30:
        L = math.exp(-m); k = 0; p = r.random()
        while p > L:
            k += 1; p *= r.random()
        return k
    return max(0, round(r.gauss(m, math.sqrt(m))))

def binom(n, p, r):
    if n <= 0 or p <= 0: return 0
    if n <= 400: return sum(1 for _ in range(n) if r.random() < p)
    return min(n, max(0, round(r.gauss(n*p, math.sqrt(n*p*(1-p))))))

def clipped_low(lo, hi, r):  # Brain: unestablished, unfavourable LOW -> min(draw, centre)
    c = (lo+hi)/2; x = lo + r.random()*(hi-lo); return min(x, c)

def clipped_high(lo, hi, r):
    c = (lo+hi)/2; x = lo + r.random()*(hi-lo); return max(x, c)

def run(reply_fn, close_fn, legs=LEGS, correlated=False, seed=1, per_day=(10, 930), only=None):
    r = random.Random(seed)
    hits = 0; totals = []
    for _ in range(W):
        common = r.random()  # used only when correlated
        total = 0.0
        pd = per_day[0] + r.random()*(per_day[1]-per_day[0])
        for name, price, cd, vol, share in legs:
            if only and name not in only: continue
            days = clipped_high(*cd, r) if cd[0] != cd[1] else cd[0]
            u1 = common if correlated else r.random()
            u2 = common if correlated else r.random()
            reply = reply_fn(u1); close = close_fn(u2)
            if vol == (1, 1):
                deals = 1 if r.random() < close else 0
            else:
                sell = max(0, DAYS_LEFT - days)
                contacted = math.floor(pd*share*sell)
                inter = poisson(contacted*reply, r)
                won = binom(inter, close, r)
                v = math.floor(min(1 + r.random()*199, 100.5))
                deals = min(won, v)
            total += deals*price
        totals.append(total)
        if total >= 10000: hits += 1
    totals.sort()
    q = lambda p: totals[int(p*(W-1))]
    return hits/W, q(0.1), q(0.5), q(0.9)

# Brain's reconstructed: reply U(0,0.152) held <= 0.076; close U(0,0.52) held <= 0.26
brain_reply = lambda u: min(u*0.152, 0.076)
brain_close = lambda u: min(u*0.52, 0.26)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    print("Brain replica, all legs          ", run(brain_reply, brain_close))
    print("Brain replica, GitHub leg only   ", run(brain_reply, brain_close, only={"github"}))
    print("Brain replica, five non-GitHub   ", run(brain_reply, brain_close, only={l[0] for l in LEGS if l[0] != "github"}))
    # Realistic: positive reply 0.3-1.0% of sends (Sales.co 0.64% central), close of positives 5-20%
    real_reply = lambda u: 0.003 + u*0.007
    real_close = lambda u: 0.05 + u*0.15
    print("Realistic rates, all legs        ", run(real_reply, real_close))
    print("Realistic rates, five non-GitHub ", run(real_reply, real_close, only={l[0] for l in LEGS if l[0] != "github"}))
    print("Brain rates, correlated legs     ", run(brain_reply, brain_close, correlated=True))
    # Realistic sending capacity for a new operation: 0-300 sends/day (warm-up), not 10-930
    print("Realistic rates+capacity, non-GH ", run(real_reply, real_close, per_day=(0, 300), only={l[0] for l in LEGS if l[0] != "github"}))
    # GitHub leg modelled as what it is: one buyer, no outreach; P(valid first critical paid in window)
    print("GitHub as one buyer: see review (p << 1%, not a funnel)")
