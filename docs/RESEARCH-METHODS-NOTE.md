# Research methods note: what the literature supports, and what Brain changes

**Scope (2026-10-18).** This is a bounded review written to inform the
implementation of `docs/OPPORTUNITY-INTELLIGENCE-PLAN.md` §3.3 and §3.5. It is
not a survey. I read the Anthropic multi-agent research post and DeepSearchQA in
full. I then read primary papers or abstracts on:

- adaptive retrieval depth (Adaptive-RAG);
- perspective-guided question generation (STORM);
- expected-information-gain question selection (Uncertainty of Thoughts);
- citation quality (DeepResearch Bench FACT, SAFE);
- over-search and under-search (Search Wisely, To Search or Not to Search);
- failure taxonomies of deep-research agents (DEFT/FINDER);
- learning from experience (Agent Workflow Memory, Reflexion);
- link rot (Pew 2024).

Every host I tried was reachable. Some pages gave only the abstract, so where a
number comes from an abstract I say so. I did not use any figure that I found
only in secondary blogs. Those include STORM's "25%" as some blogs restate it,
and Adaptive-RAG's "+5.5%". The STORM 25% quoted below is the paper's own
abstract.

One finding cuts across the rest. The measured gains are mostly about **what
gets asked and when to stop**, not about the evidence bar. Brain's gate,
standards and audit roles (§12–§14) already enforce citation correctness more
strictly than any system measured below. The adoptions are therefore all
upstream of the gate, and none of them moves a bar.

## 1. Adopted methods

| # | Method | Evidence | Brain's existing owner | The change | Expected improvement | How to test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Decisive-question-first staged screening.** Ask the kill questions before a full dive: payer, disqualifier, whether the opening is still open. | **Demonstrated, indirectly.** Adaptive-RAG routes questions by complexity. It used 2.17 retrieval steps against 4.69 for always-multi-step, and 3.60× time against 8.81×, while losing 1.8 EM (37.17 vs 39.00; FLAN-T5-XL, Table 1). Spending less on questions that do not need depth costs little accuracy. Anthropic reports "spawning 50 subagents for simple queries" as an observed failure. No paper measures kill-first screening for commercial validation specifically. | `cash/validation.ts` `startValidations` / `validationQuestion`; `compilerProfiles.ts`. There is no stage split today. Every dive asks all 16 lanes. | Plan §3.3(a): add a stage-1 profile `COMMERCIAL_VALIDATION_SCREEN` with the `payer`, `DISQUALIFIER_LANE` and still-open lanes only. It runs under the same envelope and the same per-lane floors. An opening that fails stage 1 goes DORMANT with typed reopen conditions. | Fragments per opening drop sharply for dead openings. Production's 89-of-901 acceptance rate should rise, because fewer full packets are spent on openings a payer question would have killed. | Unit: an opening whose stage-1 payer lane comes back NEGATIVE_EXISTENCE never gets a stage-2 packet. Readiness still needs every qualification field, so `tier.ts` tests are unchanged. Production: compare fragments per QUALIFIED opening before and after, using `refinement-report`. |
| 2 | **Value-of-information ordering, expressed lexicographically.** Ask the question whose answer could change a decision. | **Demonstrated for the principle, not the form.** UoT picks questions by expected information gain and reports +38.1% average task success over direct prompting (abstract; medical diagnosis, troubleshooting, 20Q). UoT computes a probabilistic score, which Brain may not use. What carries over is the **filter**: a question that cannot move a decision is not asked. | Already done in `cash/monetization/commission.ts` `decisiveCriteria`/`feeds()`, `intelligence/uncertainty.ts` `rank()` (invalidating → consequence → dependents) and `quota.ts` tiers. **Gap:** card-field research needs (`reconcileDiscoverableGaps`) ignore this filter and raise a need for every blank. | Plan §3.3(c): raise a need only for fields on the opportunity's current `tier.toAdvance` rung. Order dives lexicographically: reconsidered, then could cross a tier boundary, then kill question first, then known-cheap, then partly answered, then arrival order. A blank on a later rung reads `WAITING{on: FIELD:<earlier>}`. **No probability, no weights.** | Fewer research needs per opportunity, and a researched field more often moves a tier. | Unit: a later-rung blank raises no need until the earlier rung clears. Test that the ordering is a pure comparator over rows (no number is computed). Production: the share of settled needs that changed `tier` within the next tick. |
| 3 | **Reuse accepted claims before new search.** | **Demonstrated.** Search Wisely: one agentic-RAG model "could have avoided searching in 27.7% of its search steps" (abstract). Anthropic reports duplicated subagent work as a top failure. This repo measured the same thing: own-project gated claims are invisible to the archive check (plan §2.4). | `reconcile/coverage.ts` `projectClaims` → `assessRequirement`; `russell/coverage.ts` `coverBeforeWork`. §31 shared findings already reuse claims across projects. | Plan §3.5(1): `projectClaims` adds the project's own `citableClaims` as `ExistingClaim` VERIFIED. SATISFIED is unchanged: two publishers, strength ≥ 0.55, relevance ≥ 0.5. §3.5(5): memoize the claim set per project per tick under `archiveMarker`. | The largest single waste named in the plan. Requirements already settled stop spawning fragments. | `tests/researchReuse.test.ts`: own claims satisfy a requirement with two publishers, and not with one. A counter shows one archive read per project per tick. `sharedKnowledge.test.ts` stays green. |
| 4 | **Per-host reachability memory and alternate-source fallback.** | **Demonstrated that the condition is common.** Pew 2024: 38% of 2013 pages and about 25% of 2013–2023 pages were inaccessible by October 2023, and 21–23% of government and news pages hold at least one broken link. Anthropic: source-selection heuristics in prompts fixed a measured bias toward SEO farms, so naming better sources in the prompt works. No paper measures per-host memory. | `research/surfaceRecovery.ts` `recoverFragmentAfterSurfaceChange` (operator-only today); `repair.ts` `LADDER` (no rung for an unreadable source). SURFACE_PROBE readings exist. | Plan §3.5(2–4): store `block_cause`/`block_host`. Add an `UNREADABLE` ladder: canonical link, then archived copy, then official filings, then a different repository, then `MARK_UNRESOLVED`. `hostReachability()` is derived from `retrieval_state` × `executor_routine_id`. The prompt names hosts this surface failed to reach. Auto-recovery runs once per (fragment, RETRIEVED reading) with the existing guards. | Fewer of the 62 SOURCES_UNREADABLE and the egress-caused blocks repeat the same dead host, and recovery needs no operator. | `researchUnreadableRepair.test.ts`: the ladder respects the envelope. Recovery fires once per reading, never on a terminal packet, and the ceiling is raised rather than reset. |
| 5 | **Breadth first, then depth only when justified.** | **Demonstrated qualitatively; partly measured.** Anthropic's agents "start with short, broad queries … then narrow". It reports token usage explained 80% of BrowseComp variance, so effort matters. STORM's broader question generation gave +25% organization and +10% breadth over an outline-RAG baseline (abstract). | Depth is `intelligence/depth.ts` `allocateDepth` (a lexicographic cascade that only raises). Breadth is decomposition in `splitting.ts`/`planCoverageFragments`. **Gap:** `prompts.ts` gives no search-strategy instruction (no "broad first, then narrow"). | A short instruction in the fragment prompt: broad short queries first, survey what exists, then open full primary sources. This is prompt text only. Depth stays with `allocateDepth`; nothing new decides depth. | Fewer empty-result searches and fewer REQUIRED_LANE_EMPTY blocks (98 in 7 days). | Prompt-snapshot test that the instruction is present. Production: the REQUIRED_LANE_EMPTY share before and after, from the stored `block_cause`. |
| 6 | **Explicit task boundaries for parallel work.** | **Demonstrated.** Anthropic: parallel subagents and parallel tool calls cut research time "by up to 90%". Vague briefs caused duplicated searches. Each subagent needs an objective, an output format, source guidance and boundaries. Brain already sends these on every fragment. | `bundling.ts` `bundleFragments`/`assertSeparable` (separate claims per fragment, discard unseparable output); fragment declarations (§12); the fleet. | **No new mechanism.** Plan §3.4: concurrency becomes min(grant's free slots − 1, routable surfaces, 0 if SHEDDING). Also add the other fragments' bounded questions to each fragment's prompt as "not yours". That last item is the one cheap addition. | Less overlap between sibling fragments in one packet. | Unit: the fragment prompt lists sibling questions. Production: count duplicate source URLs across sibling fragments, using `duplicateGroups`. |
| 7 | **Explicit stopping when evidence suffices.** | **Demonstrated as a real failure mode.** Anthropic lists "continuing after sufficient results were found" and "endlessly searching for sources that don't exist" as failures. DeepSearchQA names dynamic stopping "perhaps the most critical capability", and its 13–15 point F1-versus-fully-correct gap is partly over-answering (8–10% "correct + extraneous"). To Search or Not to Search reports that outcome-only reward cuts under-search but raises over-search. | Already done: `intelligence/sufficiency.ts` `assessSufficiency`/`mayProceedToSynthesis`; `MAX_FRAGMENT_ATTEMPTS`; `repair.ts` → `MARK_UNRESOLVED`; `NEGATIVE_EXISTENCE` (§14). | **Small:** apply the same rule within a fragment. When a stage-1 kill lane is answered NOT_MET, the dive stops (from #1). The fragment prompt states that a documented absence is a complete answer for the lane. | Fewer attempts spent on non-existent sources. | Unit: a fragment whose required lane carries a valid NEGATIVE_EXISTENCE claim does not mint a repair. |
| 8 | **Retrospective-driven adaptation, as a reading, not a rule.** | **Demonstrated in agents, not in research pipelines.** Agent Workflow Memory: +24.6% and +51.1% relative success on Mind2Web/WebArena, with fewer steps (abstract). Reflexion: 91% vs 80% HumanEval pass@1. Both feed past outcomes into prompts, not into gates. | `intelligence/retrospective.ts` (row-derived DOMAIN/GENERAL lessons, shown to readers, never gating); `repair.ts` `ecosystemsTried`/`terminologyFromEvidence`. | Put the top DOMAIN lessons and the host-reachability reading (#4) into the planning and fragment prompts as context. This is AWM's mechanism. The order of the repair `LADDER` stays fixed. A lesson informs the worker and never changes the strategy order, the bar or the depth. | Repeat failures of the same kind fall in later campaigns. | Unit: the prompt carries lessons and a test proves no gate input changes. Production: retrospective lessons of the same kind across successive campaigns. |

## 2. Rejected or deferred

- **Probabilistic VOI / information-gain scores** (UoT, bandit routers). Rejected: they conflict with the no-weighted-score rule. The filter survives as #2.
- **A learned complexity classifier for routing depth** (Adaptive-RAG). Rejected: it is model output deciding state. `allocateDepth`'s row-based cascade does the same job auditably.
- **Test-time multi-sampling** (DeepSearchQA: 67.2% → 85.7% fully correct at n=8). Deferred: it costs n× the fixed subscription allowance, and Brain's three-role audit already sits where a second opinion pays.
- **A separate citation agent** (Anthropic CitationAgent). Rejected as unneeded: `recordAuditEvidence` already resolves citations from extracted text, which is stronger than model attribution. FACT puts deep-research citation accuracy at 78–90%.
- **An LLM-judge 0–1 score as a quality metric.** Rejected for state. Acceptable only as an offline eval number, and never written to a row.
- **Learned source-preference weights from retrospectives.** Deferred: unproven for research pipelines, and it would let a lesson change ranking. #8 keeps it as prompt context.
- **Widening cross-project reuse beyond §31's ACCEPTED-fragment boundary.** Deferred to the owner, as plan §3.5(1) already records.
