## What the measurements say

| | fixed four slots (15:41Z) | adaptive pool |
|---|---|---|
| simultaneous jobs | 4 at most | 8 held stable (brain-a); targets reached 12 |
| accepted jobs, last hour | 5 | 15 (17:14Z), 4 (18:37Z) |
| completed jobs, last hour | 47 | 45 (17:14Z), 16 (18:37Z) |
| accepted claims (24h total) | 37 of 71 | 71 of 108 |
| packets filed (24h total) | 2 | 3 |
| who sets the number | a constant | the controller, per model, every 30 s |

The pool scales itself and backs off by itself: it grew brain-a from 2 to 12,
halved when Brain's database saturated, and since then cycles between 1 and 7 as
the free providers answer HTTP 429. That cycle is the provider's quota, not
Brain's: 31 of the last hour's calls were refused for rate, and the controller
holds each model at what its provider will take. The limits, in order:

1. **Free-provider rate limits** (HTTP 429/503) — the binding limit now. More
   free capacity means more provider keys in OmniRoute, which only the owner holds.
2. **Model-recalled sources** — the `NETWORK_ERROR` bursts were hosts the model
   invented or that are down (ENOTFOUND, connect timeouts, 404s), not a Brain
   fetch defect. The second round and Wikipedia's API help; a free search API
   would help more.
3. **Brain's own database connections** — saturation at about 12 concurrent jobs.
4. **Supply** — 2–5 jobs waiting at a time; generation now follows the free
   lane's idle capacity (#464) instead of idle Claude routines.

Cost throughout: $0. No paid call is made from the lane.
