# The brief, as it was given

This is the operator's brief for the labor kernel, preserved verbatim. It is
kept because §12 of it asks Brain to expand the model through observation, and
an instruction to revise something is worth nothing without the thing being
revised. `docs/LABOR-KERNEL.md` is what was built from it and records where the
implementation departs from it and why.

It is **not** a capability blueprint. It lives in `docs/` rather than in
`blueprints/` deliberately: `registerBlueprint` reads a source by path and
expects a document that declares faculties, and §37 records what happens when a
document of one kind is handed to a reader expecting another — a correct
statement marked FAILED for "declaring no sections this kernel recognises". No
code reads this file.

---

KERNEL: HUMAN COMPARATIVE-ADVANTAGE & LABOR ALLOCATION

PURPOSE

The Brain must not inherit a human-first operating model.

Historically, online businesses were built by identifying a service, hiring humans to perform its component tasks, organizing those humans into departments, and then automating portions of their work.

Reverse this assumption.

For every business, operation, workflow, and task:

Brain is the default production layer. Human labor is an escalation layer.

The objective is not to maximize automation for its own sake.

The objective is to continuously discover the cheapest, fastest, highest-quality, sufficiently reliable combination of:

* Brain execution
* software/tool execution
* external services
* offshore human labor
* domestic human labor
* specialized professionals
* physical-world operators

while preserving required quality, accountability, trust, legality, resilience, and customer experience.

⸻

1. HUMAN NECESSITY TEST

Before creating, preserving, or expanding any human role, determine:

1. What exact output is this human producing?
2. Can Brain currently produce that output?
3. Can Brain produce it faster?
4. Can Brain produce it cheaper?
5. Can Brain produce it at equal or higher quality?
6. Can Brain verify its own output sufficiently?
7. Can another Brain/agent independently verify the output?
8. Does execution require physical presence?
9. Does execution require licensing, certification, signature, or legally accountable human review?
10. Does human interaction itself materially contribute to conversion, trust, negotiation, relationship strength, creative judgment, or customer experience?
11. Is the human performing normal production or merely handling exceptions?
12. What prevents Brain from eliminating or shrinking this role?

If there is no defensible human comparative advantage, redesign the workflow around Brain execution.

⸻

2. DO NOT AUTOMATE THE OLD ORGANIZATION

Do not take an obsolete human workflow and merely insert AI into individual steps.

Reconstruct the workflow from first principles.

Ask:

If this business were invented today with Brain available from its first day, how would it operate?

The resulting organization may have:

* fewer departments
* fewer handoffs
* fewer managers
* fewer specialists
* dramatically fewer employees
* no traditional department at all

Brain may collapse what historically required several jobs into one autonomous workflow.

⸻

3. HUMAN ROLE CLASSES

When humans remain useful, classify the reason.

A. HUMAN INTERFACE

Human interaction itself creates value.

Examples include calling, high-value selling, negotiation, relationship management, trust-sensitive communication, and selected customer interactions.

Brain should support these humans with research, preparation, prioritization, scripts, context, monitoring, follow-up, and learning.

B. EXPERT JUDGMENT

A qualified person's judgment materially improves outcomes beyond Brain's verified capability.

Brain should perform as much preparation and analysis as possible before escalation.

The expert should receive the smallest possible high-value decision surface rather than raw work.

C. ACCOUNTABILITY / LICENSING

A human or licensed entity must legally or contractually review, approve, certify, supervise, sign, or assume responsibility.

Brain performs everything legally and operationally permissible beneath that boundary.

D. PHYSICAL EXECUTION

The task requires physical interaction with the world.

Brain plans, schedules, coordinates, monitors and verifies wherever possible while humans perform the irreducibly physical component.

E. EXCEPTION HANDLING

Brain handles the normal path.

Humans receive only cases that exceed confidence, authority, capability, or policy thresholds.

Do not staff the exception rate as though it were the normal workload.

F. OVERSIGHT / VERIFICATION

For sufficiently consequential or difficult-to-verify operations, a human may supervise or independently verify Brain output.

Oversight intensity should scale with:

* consequence of error
* reversibility
* monetary exposure
* legal exposure
* customer impact
* uncertainty
* verification quality

⸻

4. LABOR MARKET OPTIMIZATION

Once Brain establishes that a human is necessary, do not automatically assume that human should be domestic or full-time.

Determine the minimum capability required and source accordingly.

Evaluate:

* offshore contractor
* offshore employee
* specialist freelancer
* domestic contractor
* domestic employee
* licensed professional
* fractional specialist
* on-demand exception operator
* agency/vendor

Optimize for total output economics rather than nominal hourly wage.

Consider:

* quality
* communication
* reliability
* timezone
* throughput
* training cost
* supervision burden
* turnover
* error rate
* regulatory restrictions
* data-access restrictions
* customer perception
* latency

Use domestic/public-facing labor only where it produces a real advantage or is otherwise required.

⸻

5. HUMAN CAPACITY IS ELASTIC

Do not treat headcount as permanent architecture.

Human capacity should expand and contract with demonstrated workload.

Prefer:

Brain-only
→ Brain + fractional human
→ Brain + dedicated operator
→ small specialist pool
→ larger team

rather than hiring the expected mature-state organization before demand exists.

⸻

6. BRAIN SHOULD MAKE HUMANS MORE LEVERAGED

For every retained human role, ask:

How can Brain increase the amount of valuable output this person produces per hour?

Brain should remove:

* searching
* data gathering
* repetitive preparation
* administrative updates
* unnecessary documentation
* routine follow-up
* repetitive analysis
* scheduling friction
* information retrieval
* manual status reporting

Humans should increasingly receive prepared situations requiring the thing humans remain best positioned to contribute.

⸻

7. CONTINUOUS ROLE COMPRESSION

Human necessity is not permanent.

Maintain a capability map containing:

* role
* tasks
* reason human currently exists
* Brain capability required to replace/augment it
* current automation percentage
* human cost
* Brain/tool cost
* throughput
* error rate
* quality
* exception frequency
* replacement blockers

Periodically rerun the Human Necessity Test.

When Brain gains a capability that eliminates a blocker:

1. test the Brain pathway;
2. compare performance;
3. migrate gradually;
4. preserve rollback;
5. reduce unnecessary human workload;
6. reallocate strong humans to remaining comparative-advantage work where useful.

The organization should naturally become more Brain-native as Brain improves.

⸻

8. BUSINESS CREATION

When evaluating a new revenue business, do NOT begin with:

"What team does this business require?"

Begin with:

"What valuable external outcome can Brain sell and reliably deliver?"

Then determine the irreducible human remainder.

A historically labor-intensive business may become attractive precisely because Brain collapses its labor structure.

Therefore, continually reevaluate old industries under current Brain capabilities.

The opportunity is not merely inventing AI businesses.

It is discovering conventional businesses whose economics become radically better when Brain replaces most of their historical production cost.

⸻

9. BUSINESS / CAPABILITY SEPARATION

Do not confuse a business with its production capability.

Multiple businesses may consume the same Brain capabilities.

Examples:

research capability
document generation
software construction
financial processing
media transformation
workflow execution
communications
monitoring
analysis
verification

Build reusable capabilities centrally and allow many businesses to consume them.

Do not recreate the same capability separately inside every company.

⸻

10. EXTERNAL HUMAN NETWORK

Maintain access to human capability without necessarily maintaining permanent payroll.

Build pools of verified operators and specialists categorized by:

* capability
* jurisdiction
* language
* location
* cost
* availability
* performance
* credentials
* reliability
* permitted data/access level

Brain should be capable of determining:

"I cannot efficiently or safely complete this component internally. I require human capability X for approximately Y hours/output."

It should then route that requirement to the appropriate human layer.

⸻

11. MEASUREMENT

For every workflow track:

* Brain execution %
* human execution %
* human exception %
* cost per completed output
* time per output
* error/rework rate
* customer outcome
* human hours consumed
* Brain/tool cost
* bottleneck
* confidence
* verification coverage

The goal is not "zero humans."

The goal is:

minimum necessary human effort per unit of high-quality economic output.

⸻

12. SELF-EXPANSION

This kernel is intentionally incomplete.

Brain must expand it through observation.

When Brain encounters:

* a new reason humans remain necessary
* a task humans consistently outperform Brain at
* a new Brain capability that makes an existing role obsolete
* a new labor-market advantage
* a better verification mechanism
* a recurring exception
* a new business made viable by automation
* an unexpected failure of Brain-only execution

it should update its model of human comparative advantage.

Do not preserve assumptions simply because they were true when this kernel was created.

⸻

13. OPERATOR VISIBILITY

Externally, surface:

HUMAN DEPENDENCIES

What humans currently do and why they remain necessary.

AUTOMATION FRONTIER

Which human responsibilities Brain is close to absorbing.

BOTTLENECKS

What currently prevents additional Brain execution.

HUMAN CAPACITY NEEDS

Where additional human capability would materially increase revenue or throughput.

ROLE COMPRESSION

Where Brain improvements have reduced human workload.

NEW BUSINESS ECONOMICS

Businesses whose economics have materially improved because Brain can now perform work previously requiring humans.

Do not overwhelm the operator with routine internal execution.

Surface decisions, constraints, opportunities, material changes, and required authority.

⸻

PRIME DIRECTIVE

Never ask:

"How many people would a normal company hire to do this?"

Ask:

"What is the smallest amount of human capability required after Brain has done everything it can reliably, economically, and appropriately do?"

Then continuously attempt to make that remainder smaller.

## Where the implementation departs from this, and why

Four places, each recorded here rather than left for a reader to notice.

**§7 asks for a "current automation percentage" on the capability map, and there
is none.** Every figure this kernel reports is counted from rows or reads
`UNKNOWN` (invariant 33), and a percentage of *tasks* would read as a percentage
of work — which nothing measures. What is reported instead is the count, with
its denominator named.

**§11 asks for cost per output, time per output, error rate and human hours, and
all four report `UNKNOWN`.** Brain holds no row for any of them. They are
reported rather than omitted, each naming what would measure it, because an
absent line reads as *nothing to say about it*.

**§1's question 12 is derived rather than recorded.** The brief asks it as a
question to be answered; it is answered here from the other eleven and from the
capability reading, so that a blocker clears itself when the thing it names
stops being true.

**§5 and §10 are not built.** Elastic capacity and a pool of verified external
operators are a roster of real people with contact details and credentials, and
nothing in this kernel records a person's name or reaches anybody. What is built
is the half that decides *whether* a person is needed and *what published
sources say that capability costs*. Building the roster is a separate decision
with its own authorization, and the envelope here forbids every action it would
require.
