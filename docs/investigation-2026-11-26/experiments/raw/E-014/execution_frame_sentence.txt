Look across the whole economy, not only at work this operation can already do: business and consumer markets, goods and services, brokerage, matchmaking, subcontracting, logistics, sourcing, resale, arbitrage and running operations for others are all in scope, and these are examples rather than a limit. For context, today this operation runs online with AI workers and delivers research reports and these kinds of work: spreadsheet, business document, presentation, data operations, website, programming, graphic design, 3D model; it holds no professional licence, certification, bonding, government vendor registration or local physical presence, and takes no employment or hourly positions; it holds no capital of its own to commit, so say for each opening how much money it needs up front and whether it is paid before any cost falls due; and it is paid online, through a checkout or invoice route a buyer already uses. That describes where it starts, not what is worth reporting. For each opening name who would pay this operation, roughly how much, how the money would reach it, the earliest it could arrive, and whether it could act on it now. An opening it could not act on today is still worth reporting when the money is real: name the one licence, registration, capability, partner or amount of capital it would need rather than leaving it out. Leave out job postings, hourly positions and anything already closed. Leave out individual project listings on freelance marketplaces: this operation does not take freelance work. A company publishing its own prices or fees is evidence of what that company charges, not of anybody paying this operation, so on its own it is not an opening. A kind of business that publicly and repeatedly pays for something this operation delivers, with a published place to find those businesses and their email addresses, is an opening, and the prices others charge them are its price evidence. This operation is run entirely online, with AI and software doing as much of the work as possible: the owner does no physical work — no travel, no deliveries, no distributing anything, no meeting customers, no performing a service and no running a physical operation. Work that is physical is still in scope when an independent supplier or subcontractor performs it and this operation coordinates it remotely, as a broker, referrer or subcontracting coordinator. Look in every country and region: where the buyers are is a business choice made on demand, margin, fulfilment access, how fast money settles, competition and how much can be automated — never on where the owner lives.
