Which ways could bring this operation money? The owner offered their own work for this goal ("I can put in 15 hours"), so look first for work buyers book and pay the owner for directly, in person included, using what their words say they have — then for work independent contractors or suppliers perform while this operation sells, books, refers or subcontracts it, and for work fulfilled with AI-produced digital output. For each opening give: the kind of buyer and where they are found, a dated sign that buyers want it now, the price buyers are shown paying for it, how many such buyers there are and how often each buys, who performs or produces the work and how it is delivered, what it costs to get each customer, the cost per sale, and how quickly the money is collected. This is for a short cash sprint whose goal is: Seattle. I have zero money to invest and I need $500 within 7 days. I can put in 15 hours this week. Figure out the fastest legit way. Prefer markets for their economics and for how much of the work can be run remotely; do not default to any one place, including wherever the owner is. Look across the whole economy, not only at work this operation can already do: business and consumer markets, goods and services, brokerage, matchmaking, subcontracting, logistics, sourcing, resale, arbitrage and running operations for others are all in scope, and these are examples rather than a limit. For context, today this operation runs online with AI workers and delivers research reports and these kinds of work: spreadsheet, business document, presentation, data operations, website, programming, graphic design, 3D model; it holds no professional licence, certification, bonding, government vendor registration or local physical presence, and takes no employment or hourly positions; it holds no capital of its own to commit, so say for each opening how much money it needs up front and whether it is paid before any cost falls due; and it is paid online, through a checkout or invoice route a buyer already uses. That describes where it starts, not what is worth reporting. For each opening name who would pay this operation, roughly how much, how the money would reach it, the earliest it could arrive, and whether it could act on it now. An opening it could not act on today is still worth reporting when the money is real: name the one licence, registration, capability, partner or amount of capital it would need rather than leaving it out. Leave out job postings, hourly positions and anything already closed. Leave out individual project listings on freelance marketplaces: this operation does not take freelance work. A company publishing its own prices or fees is evidence of what that company charges, not of anybody paying this operation, so on its own it is not an opening. A kind of business that publicly and repeatedly pays for something this operation delivers, with a published place to find those businesses and their email addresses, is an opening, and the prices others charge them are its price evidence. This operation is run entirely online, with AI and software doing as much of the work as possible: the owner does no physical work — no travel, no deliveries, no distributing anything, no meeting customers, no performing a service and no running a physical operation. Work that is physical is still in scope when an independent supplier or subcontractor performs it and this operation coordinates it remotely, as a broker, referrer or subcontracting coordinator. Look in every country and region: where the buyers are is a business choice made on demand, margin, fulfilment access, how fast money settles, competition and how much can be automated — never on where the owner lives.
