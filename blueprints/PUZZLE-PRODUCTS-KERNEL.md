# Puzzle Products + Production Kernel

The operator's order, committed verbatim so that its own sentences reach a
worker rather than only its sha-256.

§39 records why this file exists in this form. A programme that names a
blueprint and stores the hash of its bytes has proved **integrity** and
nothing else: integrity says the file has not changed, and says nothing about
whether one word of it ever reached a worker. So `services/puzzle/directive.ts`
parses the sections below, and the questions this kernel asks carry the
directive's own words. A section that goes missing is a boot-visible failure
rather than a question that reads almost right.

Two things this file is **not**. It is not a list of puzzle formats that
bounds the universe — the formats below are named as a *seed to search from*,
in the order's own words, and the kernel discovers the rest from evidence. And
it is not an authorization: every effect on the world named anywhere below is a
`COMMERCIAL_ACTION` a person grants separately, and nothing in this kernel can
reach one.

---

## 1. The mechanism, stated generally

Brain already holds every part of a research-to-revenue loop. What it has never
held is a **reusable production system** — a thing Brain made, from which many
commercial outputs are compiled, each of which resolves back to the master that
produced it and to the validation that proved it works.

A master is not a puzzle. A puzzle instance is not a product. A product is not
a sale. Collapsing any two of those is how a kernel comes to report that it has
fifty products when it has one generator run fifty times with different covers.

## 2. The original objective, as given

### OBJECTIVE

Create the smallest complete capability that can continuously discover puzzle
opportunities; identify buyers and demand; generate or author original puzzles;
prove that they work; package them into products; publish, sell, license,
syndicate or manufacture them; measure real commercial and quality outcomes;
improve its generators, validators, products and production systems; and expand
into new puzzle formats, audiences, channels and manufacturing capabilities.

Treat puzzles as four connected businesses: a content-generation and editorial
system; a reusable intellectual-property and rights catalog; a multiformat
publishing, licensing and distribution system; and a potential physical
production and manufacturing operation.

The kernel is puzzle-first. Include puzzle-led games when they share the same
repeatable content, logic, audience, distribution or production machinery. Do
not drift into general video games merely because they are called games.

### CORE PRINCIPLE

ONE VALIDATED PUZZLE SYSTEM PRODUCES MANY QUALIFIED COMMERCIAL OUTPUTS.

A reusable master may contain generation or authoring logic, a source corpus,
lexicon, clue bank, rules or artwork, a solution engine, a difficulty model, a
page or screen architecture, a typography and design system, an answer-key
system, metadata, a validation suite, audience and channel rules, and print and
manufacturing specifications.

That master may compile into interactive web or app play, printable PDFs,
standard, pocket and large-print books, daily email or newsletter puzzles,
classroom or institutional packs, branded embeds and white-label portals,
private-label editions, recurring content feeds, cards, boxed kits or physical
products, and multiple difficulties, languages, audiences, use occasions and
channels.

### LEVERAGE

Track three different forms of leverage separately, because they are three
different questions and a single number answers none of them.

1. Master-to-SKU multiplier: qualified commercial products produced from one
   reusable system.
2. Setup-to-unit yield: physical units produced per print, tooling or machine
   setup.
3. Contribution per setup: actual gross contribution produced by each
   editorial, software, tooling or manufacturing setup.

A setup of ten producing fifty is a search target, not a quota. Ten reusable
systems may produce fifty qualified outputs only when those outputs address
real demand and differ substantively by puzzle content, mechanic, audience,
difficulty, format, use occasion, language, buyer or channel.

A cover-color change, title change, reordered pages, or other cosmetic reskin
does not create a new qualified output. Track compilations, reprints, alternate
formats, private-label editions, and new playable content honestly and
separately. Fifty is a possible ceiling, not an obligation. Release only what
passes demand, quality, rights, and contribution gates.

### VALUE CHAIN

Map and evaluate every value-capture point: demand discovery, buyer discovery,
puzzle rules and content, generation or authoring, validation, editing and
test-solving, canonical asset storage, product compilation, design and prepress,
publishing or manufacturing, packaging, distribution, transaction, fulfillment,
repeat purchase or renewal, performance learning, catalog reuse.

Do not assume that making the final consumer product is always the best economic
position. Compare creation, tooling, licensing, syndication, white-label supply,
publishing, distribution, manufacturing, contract production, software,
acquisition, and infrastructure opportunities.

### PUZZLE UNIVERSE SEED

Seed — but do not permanently limit — the universe with: crosswords and
mini-crosswords; Sudoku and number-grid variants; word searches, fill-ins,
anagrams, cryptograms, acrostics and trivia; logic grids, sequences, nonograms,
deduction puzzles and riddles; mazes, hidden-object puzzles, spot-the-difference
and activity pages; daily web puzzles, apps, newsletters, widgets, APIs and
printable downloads; puzzle books, magazines, tear-off pads, cards, travel
products and jigsaws; puzzle boxes, wooden or metal brainteasers, escape kits
and mystery kits; puzzle hunts, live competitions, corporate games and
educational puzzle products; adjacent card, board, classroom, promotional,
personalized and branded puzzle products.

Continuously expand this universe from evidence. Discover obscure formats,
underserved audiences, new mechanics, different languages, accessibility needs,
emerging channels, seasonal demand, institutional buyers, production methods and
acquisition targets.

### MONETIZATION LEDGER

Maintain a persistent ledger of every credible monetization route. Surface the
Top 5 Now prominently, but never delete or hide slower, lower-ranked, blocked,
experimental, physical-production or long-term paths.

Seed the ledger with: digital puzzle and printable sales; bundles, archives,
memberships and subscriptions; advertising, sponsorship, hints, tournaments and
premium access; apps, websites, newsletters, widgets, embeds, APIs and content
feeds; recurring syndication to newspapers, magazines, newsletters, sites and
apps; custom puzzles for brands, events, weddings, tourism, restaurants,
conferences and promotions; school, tutoring, library, museum, camp, hospitality
and senior-living programs; corporate onboarding, training, recruiting and
internal communications; white-label products and retailer-exclusive editions;
print-on-demand and conventionally printed books; wholesale, retail,
subscription boxes and private-label distribution; cards, jigsaws, boxed kits,
mechanical puzzles and activity products; generator, validation, layout,
editorial and publishing software; puzzle-catalog, newsletter, website, app or
rights acquisitions; contract assembly, personalization, finishing, printing,
packaging or manufacturing; owned machinery and spare-capacity manufacturing for
third parties.

### QUALITY STANDARD

A puzzle is not commercially valid merely because it renders attractively.

Give every puzzle an immutable identity and preserve its type, generator or
source, generation seed, version, rules, solution and proof, difficulty,
audience and locale, expected solve time, content and rights provenance,
validation result, reuse history, and product and edition relationships.

Generate the puzzle, solution, answer key and production layout from the same
canonical source so they cannot silently diverge.

Validate 100% of output with format-specific checks. Sudoku and logic puzzles:
valid construction, required solution uniqueness, valid solve path, calibrated
difficulty, and canonical duplicate detection. Crosswords: legal grid,
connectivity, correct crossings, clue-answer agreement, spelling, tense and
number consistency, acceptable fill, coherent theme, prohibited-content
screening, and calibrated difficulty. Word searches: every listed word exists
correctly, answer coordinates match, and accidental prohibited strings are
screened. Mazes: valid start, end, reachability, and intended solution
structure. All other formats: complete instructions, verified answer key,
solvability, required uniqueness, duplication checks, and format-specific
validation.

Human-edit authored crosswords and every new generator or template. Human-
playtest an appropriate stratified sample of algorithmic output for ambiguity,
readability, enjoyment, cultural fit, and actual difficulty.

If a systematic defect appears, block the batch and repair the generator or
source. Do not manually patch dozens of broken outputs and leave the source
defect alive.

### RIGHTS STANDARD

Never copy, scrape, lightly rewrite, or disguise copyrighted puzzle grids,
clues, compilations, artwork, fonts, word lists, or proprietary source material.
Preserve commercial-use rights and provenance. Screen relevant titles, branded
formats, and mechanics for trademark or licensing issues.

### PROOF LADDER

The proof ladder is: validated sample, cheap market test, first paid buyer,
repeat order or recurring feed, automated delivery, additional formats and
audiences, wholesale or licensing scale, physical-production ownership.

Prioritize routes that can reach paid proof without inventory. Paid demand
outranks views, keyword volume, competitor existence, compliments, and
hypothetical interest. Do not flood marketplaces with generic, unvalidated AI
puzzle books.

### PHYSICAL PRODUCTION LADDER

Stage 0: digital products, printables, licensing, subscriptions and custom work.
Stage 1: print-on-demand, prototypes, local digital printing and small contract
runs. Stage 2: proven outsourced batches, offset printing, wholesale pilots and
private label. Stage 3: bring personalization, finishing, assembly, packing and
quality control in-house. Stage 4: acquire printing, cutting, collating,
binding, laminating, die-cutting, packaging or product-specific machinery when
justified. Stage 5: use owned excess capacity for contract manufacturing and
adjacent products.

Treat books, cards, jigsaws, boxed games and mechanical puzzles as different
production classes. They may share intellectual property while requiring
different materials, workflows, factories, machines, safety rules, quality
systems and capital decisions.

For every production route model net collected revenue rather than retail price;
materials and components; printing, conversion, assembly and packaging; tooling,
prepress, proofs and changeovers; freight, duty, storage, fulfillment and
retailer terms; spoilage, defects, returns, markdowns and disposal; labor,
training, utilities, maintenance, insurance and floor space; throughput at the
slowest bottleneck; staffed productive utilization rather than advertised
machine speed; working capital and cash-recovery time; resale value and downside
exposure; contribution per machine-hour; and outsourcing versus ownership.

Buy machinery only when recurring aggregate demand, utilization, quality, speed,
customization, secrecy or margin makes ownership better than outsourcing on
fully loaded economics. Research and model the complete production cell, not
merely the headline printer or cutter. Keep a qualified backup supplier even
after bringing a process in-house.

### CHEAP BOOK INVESTIGATION

Explicitly reverse-engineer the Dollar General-style product: actual publisher
receipts from a roughly one-dollar retail price; retailer and distributor
shares; print quantity and production method; page count, paper, ink, binding,
trim, packaging and case packs; freight density; editorial and puzzle cost;
returns and markdowns; working capital; catalog size and release cadence;
required sell-through; and resulting contribution per book and per production
run.

Treat it as a scale, backlist and distribution problem, not automatically as the
first-cash route. Determine whether its strongest role is direct profit, a
downstream use of an already-amortized puzzle catalog, a private-label or
wholesale product, a retailer relationship builder, a utilization product for
owned equipment, or a low-margin product that should be rejected. Do not let the
cheap-book example establish the kernel's price or margin ceiling.

### AUTHORITY

Keep canonical, hypothetical, experimental and rejected states distinct. Do not
invent demand, sales, pricing, rankings, production capability, licensing
rights, vendor terms or machine economics.

Operate autonomously within existing authority. Do not silently spend money,
accept contracts, bind the user, order inventory, purchase equipment, create
paid accounts, publish under the user's identity, or expand permissions. When
such an action becomes the best next step, prepare everything possible first and
present the exact action, the exact amount or commitment, the expected result,
the downside, the deadline if real, and what will resume automatically after
approval.

Do not claim to be blocked while another meaningful route remains executable
within authority.

### MATURITY VOCABULARY

Do not falsely claim support for puzzle formats lacking real validators. Show
each format's maturity honestly: DISCOVERED, RESEARCHED, GENERATABLE,
VALIDATABLE, PRODUCTIZABLE, SELLABLE, REVENUE_PROVEN, REPEATABLE, SCALABLE,
PRODUCTION_OWNED.

### PRIME DIRECTIVE

BUILD ONE SMALL COMPLETE PUZZLE-MONEY LOOP THAT WORKS, THEN TURN EVERY VALIDATED
ASSET, TOOL, BUYER RELATIONSHIP AND PRODUCTION CAPABILITY INTO MULTIPLYING
OUTPUT — WITHOUT SACRIFICING SOLVABILITY, ORIGINALITY, ECONOMICS OR
AUDITABILITY.

## 3. How Brain holds this without turning it into a roadmap

The seed lists above are **search seeds**, and the kernel is explicit that they
do not bound the universe. `puzzle_formats` rows exist because a gated claim
named one or a person seeded one; there is no list of formats in the
repository's code, and a test reads the source to say so.

The monetization seeds are a **person's statement**, and they enter the ledger
only when a person activates the kernel — which is that person naming them,
rather than Brain inventing them. They are never deleted afterwards, whatever
their rank, because the order says so in as many words.

The maturity vocabulary is the one place this file names states rather than
questions, and it is carried into code as a declared ladder precisely so that a
format with no validator cannot be reported as one that has one.
